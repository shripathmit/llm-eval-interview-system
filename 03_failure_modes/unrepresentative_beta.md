# Unrepresentative Beta: Feedback From the Wrong Users

> Beta feedback is ground truth about your beta users. It says little about everyone else.

---

## What it is

A beta cohort is never a random sample of your users. Volunteers are more technical, more patient, and more motivated than the population that arrives at launch. When beta feedback becomes your ground truth without correction, you tune the system for the people least like your real users.

---

## How it shows up

- Beta users rate the system highly; launch users struggle with basics the beta never flagged
- Feedback concentrates on advanced features while first-run problems go unreported
- The issues beta users raise are real but rare in the full population
- Scores built on beta labels look strong until a random production sample is labeled

---

## Why it happens

Recruiting is convenience-shaped. In one voice product launch, the beta cohort was a known group given weekly tasked exercises, and the team ran launch parties where people played with a test device. That worked well for collecting real utterances and honest reactions, and the team still had to remember who was in the room: invited, guided, and enthusiastic. Unguided users at home, mid-task and impatient, are a different distribution.

---

## How to detect it

| Check | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Compare beta cohort traits to the launch population | How far the sample sits from the target |
| Label a random production sample with the same rubric | Whether beta-built scores survive contact with real traffic |
| Feedback volume by user type | Whether a small loud group is standing in for everyone |

---

## How to fix it

- Recruit the beta against the launch population's profile, not against whoever volunteers first
- Mix tasked exercises with untasked, unguided use, and weight the unguided data more
- Keep beta labels and production labels visibly separate in every report
- Re-validate the bar on a random production sample before general launch, every time
